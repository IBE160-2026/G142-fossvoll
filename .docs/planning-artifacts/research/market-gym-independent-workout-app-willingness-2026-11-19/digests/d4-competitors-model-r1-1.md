### Gamification evidence (Q2)

- Mazeas et al. (JMIR 2022) is a systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs with 2,407 participants. Gamified interventions had a small-to-medium effect on physical activity (Hedges g = 0.42, 95% CI 0.14–0.69), about +1,610 steps/day. The effect on MVPA was not significant (g = 0.31, CI −0.19 to 0.80). Gamified interventions also beat equivalent non-gamified ones. At follow-up (mean 14 weeks after the intervention ended) the effect was very small (g = 0.15). | https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8767479/ | Journal of Medical Internet Research | 2022-01 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | evidence
- The BE FIT RCT (Patel et al., JAMA Intern Med 2017; n = 200 adults from 94 families; 12-week intervention plus 12-week follow-up) used points and levels with social incentives. During the intervention, steps rose by 1,661/day vs 636 in controls. After the game ended, activity declined but stayed above control (1,385 vs 798). Fading is real but partial. | https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/effect-of-a-game-based-intervention-designed-to-enhance-social-incentives-to-increase-physical-activity-among-families-the-be-fit-randomized-clinical-trial ; https://pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2017/october/to-increase-exercise-study-shows-gaming-strategies-and-a-buddy-are-key | Penn CHIBE / Penn Medicine | 2017-10 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high | evidence
- The outcomes in this evidence base are almost all steps or general physical activity. No RCT found tests gamification for gym strength training adherence. Transferring these results to lifters is an inference. | (same two sources as above) | — | — | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | evidence
- BYU (West et al.) reviewed popular fitness apps, found that most rely on gamification, and argued that "once the novelty wears off" the motivation fades. This is an argument from analysing app design, not a measured longitudinal effect. | https://www.ctvnews.ca/health/fitness-game-apps-may-not-lead-to-long-term-success-study-1.1972761 | CTV News (reporting BYU study) | ~2014 (undated on page) | accessed 2026-11-19 | low to medium | evidence
- Sun, Dong & Jiang (Frontiers in Psychology 2025) propose an S-shaped, not linear, relationship between how many features a fitness app has and users' intention to keep exercising. More features is not always better. Only the abstract or snippet was read. | https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1671543/pdf | Frontiers in Psychology | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (not read in full) | evidence
- Claims of a "week 20" engagement decay and a "day 35" decline come from FitCraft, a vendor selling a gamified app. Neither has a traced primary source; the "week 20" claim cites BE FIT. Do not use either. | https://getfitcraft.com/science/engagement-decay | FitCraft | undated | accessed 2026-11-19 | low | evidence

### B2C model and GTM (Q3)

- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025 (about 75k apps): health and fitness is among the top categories by revenue per install (reported median about $0.63 per install at day 60, vs $0.31 overall). Only about 5% of H&F apps reach $10k total revenue within two years. Median trial-to-paid conversion is about 40%. H&F users show a strong preference for annual plans (2024 report). These figures come from secondary coverage and should be checked in the PDF. | https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/ ; https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2024/ | RevenueCat | 2025-03 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | gtm
- Adapty's 2026 benchmark puts Health & Fitness first on median one-year LTV per install, at about $1.21. Install-to-trial is about 14.5% in North America and 7.6–10.2% in other regions. The report also says about 95% of app subscription revenue goes to the top 10% of apps (winner-take-most). Adapty sells monetisation tooling. | https://adapty.io/blog/health-fitness-app-subscription-benchmarks/ ; https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/ | Adapty / PPC Land | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | gtm
- The median monthly price for Health & Fitness subscriptions is about $9.70, vs $4.99 for gaming. | https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark | Airbridge (MMP vendor) | 2026 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | pricing
- On Facebook ads, US fitness/training cost per install averaged about $19.49 (Oct 2024–Jun 2025), against a global baseline of about $11.31. Implication: paid CPI of about $10–20 against a one-year LTV of about $1.2 per install means paid-acquisition B2C is unprofitable for a typical H&F app unless conversion or retention is well above median. This is my derivation from two different datasets. | https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cost-per-app-install/fitness-training-centers/united-states | Superads | 2025 | accessed 2026-11-19 | low to medium | gtm

## Leads worth chasing

- Download the RevenueCat 2025 PDF and the Adapty 2026 H&F report directly to confirm the per-install LTV, trial conversion and retention figures for H&F.
- EGYM: Genius / "EGYM app" B2B pricing, and its corporate-wellness hybrid (EGYM Wellpass). This is the main example of a hybrid where a consumer gets gym access through an employer and gyms get paid. Not searched this run because of budget.
- Hybrid models to verify: Hevy Coach (B2B coaching tier on a consumer app), Strava-style brand or club partnerships, Liftoff's rank system and any gym partnerships it has (a SensAI comparison page mentions Liftoff "ranks").
- B2B sales-cycle length for gym software (ABC Fitness, Glofox, Mindbody): no source found. Look for SaaS founder interviews and IHRSA/HFA vendor reports.
- Apple's top-selling-titles IR page from Nintendo, for a primary Ring Fit figure.
- App-store traction (rating counts, downloads) for HeroFit, Fitness RPG, RPGFitness and LEVELING. Apple returned 429; try Sensor Tower / AppMagic or the Google Play pages.
- A systematic review of gamification specifically in resistance training, or in users who already train. Also the Frontiers 2025 S-curve paper in full.
- Qualitative evidence (Reddit r/weightroom, r/fitness, app reviews) on whether serious lifters find gamification childish or annoying.

## Looked for but not found

- A gym or strength app that shows one session's exercises as a spatial path, board or room with an avatar walking between machines. Searched: "gamified gym workout app map avatar levels" and "fitness RPG app world map workout quest". The map-based apps found are step-based (Fitness RPG, PaceQuest, Prado) or console-based (Ring Fit). The strength apps use XP, avatars or battles without a spatial layout. Absence is likely but not proven; the search covered the US App Store via web search only.
- Peer-reviewed evidence that gamification annoys or puts off experienced lifters. Nothing found. The only "fading" evidence concerns general physical activity (Mazeas 2022 follow-up g = 0.15; the BE FIT decline after the intervention).
- Primary data on B2B gym-software sales-cycle length.
- First-party traction numbers (downloads, revenue) for any of the gamified strength apps.
- Hevy's gamification features from a first-party source.
- Not searched for budget reasons: Zombies, Run!, Habitica, Ladder, Liftoff, Gymverse and EGYM pricing.
